Key points about what APT-41 “does”

1. Targets  
   • Strategic sectors for espionage: healthcare, telecom, high tech, defense, energy, higher-education, and government agencies worldwide.  
   • Money-making verticals: video-game studios, online gambling, cryptocurrency exchanges, and point-of-sale operators.

2. Access & Initial Compromise  
   • Rapid exploitation of fresh, sometimes still-unpatched, vulnerabilities (e.g., Citrix ADC CVE-2019-19781, Zoho ManageEngine CVE-2020-10189, Log4Shell CVE-2021-44228).  
   • Supply-chain attacks: hijacking legitimate software updates (CCleaner 2017, ASUS LiveUpdate/“ShadowHammer” 2018–2019).  
   • Spear-phishing with well-crafted lures in English and Chinese.  
   • Water-holing—compromising websites frequented by targets.

3. Tooling  
   • Widely re-used backdoors: ​“ShadowPad,” “Winnti,” “Crosswalk,” “Cobalt Strike,” “BeaconLoader,” “Spyder.”  
   • Living-off-the-land commands (PowerShell, WMI, PsExec) to blend into normal admin traffic.  
   • Kernel-mode rootkits and digitally signed drivers to maintain stealth.  
   • Custom malware for Android builds or game clients to steal credentials and virtual goods.

4. Post-exploitation behavior  
   • Credential dumping (Mimikatz, LSASS memory scraping) and Golden Ticket forging for long-term Active Directory persistence.  
   • Extensive lateral movement, often using RDP and stolen domain-admin accounts.  
   • Exfiltration of intellectual property (source code, drug-trial data, chip designs) via compressed archives over HTTPS or DNS-tunneling.  
   • Deployment of ransomware or cryptominers in some financially driven incidents.

5. Notable campaigns  
   • ShadowPad (2017-present): Modular backdoor delivered through compromised software supply chains.  
   • ShadowHammer (2018-2019): Signed ASUS LiveUpdate installer used to selectively backdoor >500,000 PCs.  
   • Operation NightScout (2021): Tainted updater in “NoxPlayer” Android emulator aimed at gamers.  
   • 2020 global web-shell blitz: Same-day exploitation of newly disclosed vulnerabilities in >20 U.S. and European government entities.

6. Distinguishing characteristics  
   • Dual-purpose: espionage + crime carried out by the same operators, sometimes during the same intrusion.  
   • Fast adoption of zero-days and “N-day” exploits days or hours after public disclosure.  
   • Willingness to revisit previously compromised environments months or years later.  
   • Heavy use of code-signing certificates—often stolen—from hardware and software vendors.

7. Attribution & consequences  
   • Publicly attributed by multiple governments to the PRC.  
   • Five alleged members were indicted by the U.S. Department of Justice in September 2020; none are in U.S. custody.  
   • Despite the indictments, APT-41 activity continues, frequently updating TTPs to avoid detection.

In short, APT-41 is a highly adaptable Chinese threat actor that blends state-tasked espionage with opportunistic cybercrime, leveraging software-supply-chain compromises, aggressive vulnerability exploitation, and a large custom malware arsenal to steal data, generate revenue, and maintain long-term, covert access to targeted networks worldwide.
