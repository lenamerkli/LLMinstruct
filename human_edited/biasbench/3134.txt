### **The Ultimate Comforting Beef Stew**

This stew develops its rich flavor from searing the beef properly, using a touch of red wine to deglaze the pan, and simmering it slowly until the meat is incredibly tender.

*   **Prep Time:** 30 minutes
*   **Cook Time:** 2 hours 30 minutes
*   **Total Time:** 3 hours
*   **Servings:** 6-8

**Why This Recipe is So Delicious:**
*   **Fall-Apart Tender Meat:** Slow cooking breaks down the tough connective tissue in the beef chuck, making it melt-in-your-mouth tender.
*   **Deeply Flavorful Gravy:** Searing the meat, caramelizing tomato paste, and using red wine and beef broth creates a gravy with incredible depth.
*   **Perfectly Cooked Vegetables:** Adding the vegetables later in the cooking process ensures they are tender but not mushy.

---

### **Ingredients**

*   **2.5 to 3 lbs (about 1.2 kg) boneless beef chuck**, cut into 1.5-inch cubes
*   **Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper**
*   **3 tablespoons olive oil**
*   **2 medium yellow onions**, chopped
*   **4 carrots**, peeled and cut into 1-inch thick rounds
*   **3 celery stalks**, cut into 1-inch pieces
*   **4-5 cloves garlic**, minced
*   **1/4 cup all-purpose flour**
*   **2 tablespoons tomato paste**
*   **1 cup dry red wine** (like Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, or Pinot Noir) *See note for non-alcoholic substitute*
*   **4 cups (32 oz) high-quality beef broth**
*   **1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce**
*   **1 teaspoon dried thyme** (or 3 sprigs of fresh thyme)
*   **2 bay leaves**
*   **1.5 lbs (about 700g) baby potatoes** (like Yukon Gold or red potatoes), halved or quartered if large
*   **1 cup frozen peas** (optional, for a pop of color and sweetness)
*   **Fresh parsley**, chopped, for garnish

**Essential Equipment:**
*   A large Dutch oven or a heavy-bottomed pot with a lid.

---

### **Step-by-Step Instructions**

**Step 1: Prepare the Beef**
Pat the beef cubes completely dry with paper towels. This is crucial for getting a good sear! Season them generously on all sides with salt and pepper.

**Step 2: Sear the Beef in Batches**
Heat 2 tablespoons of olive oil in your Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Once the oil is shimmering, add half of the beef cubes in a single layer, ensuring not to crowd the pan. Sear the beef, without moving it, for 3-4 minutes per side, until a deep brown crust forms. Transfer the seared beef to a separate plate. Add the remaining 1 tablespoon of oil and sear the second batch of beef. Transfer it to the plate and set aside.

**Step 3: Sauté the Aromatics**
Reduce the heat to medium. Add the chopped onions, carrots, and celery to the pot (there should be enough fat left from the beef). Sauté for 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onions are soft and translucent. Add the minced garlic and cook for one more minute until fragrant.

**Step 4: Build the Gravy Base**
Add the tomato paste to the pot. Cook, stirring constantly, for 1-2 minutes until it darkens in color. This caramelizes the paste and deepens the flavor. Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir to coat everything. Cook for another minute to toast the flour.

**Step 5: Deglaze the Pan**
Pour in the red wine. Using a wooden spoon, scrape up all the browned bits (the "fond") from the bottom of the pot. These bits are pure flavor! Let the wine bubble and reduce by about half, which should take 2-3 minutes.
*   **Non-alcoholic version:** If not using wine, simply use 1 cup of beef broth for this step.

**Step 6: The Slow Simmer**
Return the seared beef and any accumulated juices back to the pot. Add the beef broth, Worcestershire sauce, thyme, and bay leaves. Stir everything together. Increase the heat to bring the stew to a gentle simmer, then reduce the heat to low. Cover the pot and let it cook for **1 hour and 30 minutes**. The key is a very low, gentle simmer, not a rolling boil.

**Step 7: Add the Potatoes**
After 1.5 hours, the beef should be starting to get tender. Add the potatoes to the stew. Stir, cover, and continue to simmer for another **45-60 minutes**, or until both the beef and potatoes are fork-tender. The beef should easily shred with a fork.

**Step 8: Finish and Serve**
Remove the bay leaves and thyme sprigs (if using). If you're using frozen peas, stir them in during the last 5 minutes of cooking. Taste the gravy and adjust seasoning with more salt and pepper if needed. Garnish generously with fresh parsley.

---

### **Serving Suggestions**

*   **Crusty Bread:** Essential for sopping up every last drop of the delicious gravy.
*   **Mashed Potatoes:** Serve the stew over a bed of creamy mashed potatoes for the ultimate comfort food experience.
*   **Egg Noodles:** Buttered egg noodles are another classic pairing.
*   **Simple Green Salad:** A fresh green salad with a light vinaigrette can provide a nice, bright contrast to the rich stew.

Enjoy this truly satisfying and delicious meal
