### **Pan-Seared Kangaroo Fillet with Red Wine & Juniper Sauce**

This gourmet-style dish is perfect for a special dinner and comes together surprisingly quickly.

**Serves:** 2
**Prep time:** 10 minutes
**Cook time:** 15 minutes

---

#### **Ingredients:**

*For the Kangaroo:*
*   400g (14 oz) kangaroo fillet or backstrap (usually 2 pieces)
*   1 tbsp olive oil
*   1 tsp cracked black pepper
*   1 tsp sea salt

*For the Red Wine & Juniper Sauce:*
*   1 tbsp olive oil
*   1 small shallot, finely chopped
*   1 clove garlic, minced
*   6-8 juniper berries, lightly crushed with the back of a knife
*   1 sprig fresh rosemary (or 1 tsp dried)
*   ½ cup (125ml) full-bodied red wine (e.g., Shiraz or Cabernet Sauvignon)
*   ½ cup (125ml) beef stock
*   1 tbsp balsamic vinegar
*   1 tsp brown sugar
*   1 tbsp cold butter, cubed
*   Salt and pepper to taste

*Optional for serving:*
*   Creamy mashed potatoes
*   Steamed green beans or asparagus
*   Roasted baby carrots

---

#### **Instructions:**

1.  **Prepare the Meat:** Take the kangaroo fillets out of the fridge about 15 minutes before cooking to bring them to room temperature. This helps them cook evenly. Pat them completely dry with paper towels. Rub them with the olive oil and generously season all over with salt and pepper.

2.  **Make the Sauce Base:** While the meat rests, start the sauce. Heat 1 tbsp of olive oil in a medium saucepan over medium heat. Add the chopped shallot and cook for 2-3 minutes until softened. Add the garlic, crushed juniper berries, and rosemary. Cook for another minute until fragrant.

3.  **Reduce the Sauce:** Pour in the red wine and balsamic vinegar. Add the brown sugar. Bring to a boil and let it simmer vigorously for 3-4 minutes, until reduced by about half. This cooks out the alcohol and concentrates the flavor.
    Add the beef stock, bring back to a simmer, and reduce again for another 5-7 minutes, until the sauce coats the back of a spoon. Turn the heat to very low.

4.  **Cook the Kangaroo:** Heat a heavy-based skillet (cast iron is ideal) over a very high heat until it is smoking hot. Add the kangaroo fillets. **Do not move them** for 2 minutes to get a perfect sear.
    Flip and cook for another 2 minutes for medium-rare (this is ideal for kangaroo). For medium, cook for 3 minutes per side. **Avoid overcooking**, as it will become tough and dry.
    Remove the fillets from the pan and place them on a warm plate. **Let them rest for at least 5 minutes.** This is crucial for tender meat.

5.  **Finish the Sauce:** While the meat rests, finish the sauce. Remove the rosemary sprig and juniper berries if you prefer a smoother sauce (you can leave them for more intense flavor). Whisk the cold butter into the reduced sauce, one cube at a time, until it melts and creates a slightly glossy, thickened sauce. Season with salt and pepper to taste.

6.  **Serve:** Slice the rested kangaroo fillet against the grain into 1cm thick medallions. Fan them out on warmed plates. Drizzle the red wine and juniper sauce over and around the meat. Serve immediately with your chosen sides.

---

### **Chef's Tips & Important Notes:**

*   **Don't Overcook!** Kangaroo is best served medium-rare to rare. Well-done kangaroo will be very tough. The inside should be a deep pinkish-red.
*   **Resting is Non-Negotiable:** Resting allows the muscle fibers to relax and reabsorb their juices, ensuring a juicy result.
*   **Juniper Berries:** These are the classic pairing for game meats. If you can't find them, you can use a tablespoon of gin (the main flavoring in gin is juniper!) – add it with the red wine and let it boil off.
*   **Marinating:** For an even more tender result, you can marinate the fillets for 2-4 hours in a mixture of olive oil, red wine, a smashed garlic clove, and a bay leaf. Pat completely dry before searing.
*   **Alternative Cuts:** If you have kangaroo diced for stews, brown it well and then slow-cook it for hours in a liquid (like a rich stock with vegetables) to make a beautiful, tender ragù or stew.
