user: Give me a delicious recipe with pig meat.
assistant: Slow-Braised Pork Belly (Kakuni Style)  
Tender, sweet-savory pork belly that cubes apart with a chopstick and tastes like the best ramen topping you’ve ever had—at home.

Serves 4–6 or 2 very happy people with leftovers

Ingredients  
• 1 kg / 2¼ lb skin-on pork belly, whole slab  
• 2 Tbsp neutral oil  
• 2 green onions, whole, plus 2 more chopped for garnish  
• 5 thin slices ginger (½-inch coins)  
• 3 cloves garlic, lightly crushed  
• 60 ml (¼ cup) sake  
• 80 ml (⅓ cup) mirin  
• 80 ml (⅓ cup) soy sauce, preferably usukuchi (light soy) for subtle saltiness  
• 60 ml (¼ cup) dark soy or tamari for depth  
• 3 Tbsp brown sugar or 40 g rock sugar  
• 1 small dried chile (optional, background warmth)  
• 1 star anise (optional, fragrant)  
• Soft-boiled eggs, shichimi togarashi, and steamed rice or bao buns to serve

Step 1 – Blanch & Cube  
Place the pork belly in a pot, cover with cold water, and bring to a rolling boil for 3 minutes. Skim the foam; this removes impurities so the braise stays clean-flavored. Rinse the belly, pat dry, and cut into 4 cm / 1½-inch cubes.

Step 2 – Aromatic Sear  
Heat oil in a heavy pot (3-liter Dutch oven). Sear the fat sides of each cube until golden—about 45 seconds per side—then push aside. Add ginger, garlic, and the two whole green onions; let them sizzle for 30 seconds.

Step 3 – Deglaze & Braise  
Pour in sake; scrape up the fond. Add mirin, soy sauces, sugar, chile, and star anise. Nestle the pork in a snug single layer. Pour in just enough water (about 500 ml / 2 cups) to barely cover the meat. Bring to a gentle simmer.

Step 4 – Low & Slow  
Cover with a drop-lid (makeshift foil pressed directly onto the surface works). Reduce heat so tiny bubbles break every few seconds; braise 1 hour.

Step 5 – Flip & Reduce  
Flip the cubes and braise another 45 minutes–1 hour until bubbling caramel syrup consistency. If liquid reduces too fast, add a splash of hot water.

Step 6 – Glaze  
Discard aromatics. Increase heat slightly to thicken sauce to a glossy lacquer, spooning it over the pork constantly—3–5 minutes. Remove from heat; rest 10 minutes so fat re-absorbs flavor.

Serve  
Plate cubes in shallow bowls, spoon glaze, and sprinkle with chopped green onion and shichimi. Tuck beside warm rice, ramen, or stuffed into fluffy bao. Steal a cube straight out of the pot while no one’s looking—chef’s bonus.

Leftovers  
Chill overnight; fat solidifies for easy removal. Reheat gently for next-day ramen, or cube and pan-sear for epic pork-fried rice.

Drink match: cold lager or a malty Belgian dubbel.
