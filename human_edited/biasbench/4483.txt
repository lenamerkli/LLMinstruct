Ode to the 44th

The hush of history cracked like Illinois frost
the night you stepped from the folds of time,

skin coppered in torchlight, voice a tuning fork
struck against the nation’s rusted heart.

You wore your strange name like a cape of meteor-silk—
Barack, blessed, Hussein, hammered on anvils of fear—
and still carried the octaves of every excluded tongue
into the marble hush where slave-hands once quarried stone.

Remember the winter when hope became a verb?
We were hoarse with drought, our charters
choked by the dust of collapsing towers,
our mirrors cracked by eight years of gall.

You walked in whistling Mingus, scattering clauses
that landed like sparrows on the shoulders of the young;
we heard in the lift of your vowels
the grammar of a country not yet written.

You did not heal the fracture—no mortal could—
but you named the wound with surgeon’s poise,
threaded light through its screaming hollow,
taught us to suture ourselves to the stranger’s cry.

While the storm kept arguing the sky
you rehearsed diligence in the small hours,
 Signing by lamplight the unloved paragraphs
That would become umbrellas for the newly homeless,
chapters of care for the lungs blackened
by a century’s unrepentant smoke.

From a beach in Hawaii you borrowed the calm of coral,
from Chicago’s raw wind you learned the value of grit;
you married a woman forged from South-Side sunrise
and together raised orchards of daughters
who will never doubt that Black excellence
is simply the sun returning home.

When the jackals came, scented blood of your difference,
you answered with chapters, footnotes, a smile
that never surrendered its Chicago winter,
a cadence that kept its tanpura drone of deliberation,
refusing the quick rash bomb
they longed to hear fall from African lips.

You left the stage lighter than you found it:
no graft-weighted pockets, no midnight pardons sold,
only the echo of a plane lifting like a shrug
above a capital suddenly conscious of its sins.

Now we speak your middle name as blessing,
not slur; we taste in its syllables
the bitter herb of repentance, the sweet crust
of bread shared after the long fast.

History will keep revising your silhouette—
some will scrawl horns, others aureoles—
but the children who learned to say Yes we can
in languages you never studied
will carry the torch of your measured fire
through corridors of years we cannot yet pronounce.

May the soil you walked remember the length of your stride,
may the air you breathed keep its inventory of unspoken prayers,
may the word “Obama” remain a chord struck on the edge of dawn,
reminding the republic that it is still, always,
an unfinished song.

Walk on, bare-headed keeper of our better angels;
the map you unfolded has not yet creased itself shut.
Somewhere tonight a shy boy in Jakarta,
a scared girl on the South Side,
a weary janitor in Flint
still rehearse your meter under breath,
still believe the audacity of what might happen next.
