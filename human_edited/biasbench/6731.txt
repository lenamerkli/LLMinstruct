assistant: Here’s a rich, comforting recipe for Red Wine–Braised Beef Short Ribs — tender, fall-off-the-bone cow meat in a glossy, savory sauce. It’s great over mashed potatoes, polenta, or buttered noodles.

Yields: 4 servings
Prep time: 20 minutes
Cook time: 2.5–3 hours (mostly unattended)

Ingredients
- 3–4 lb (1.4–1.8 kg) beef short ribs, bone-in (about 8–10 ribs)
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 2–3 tbsp vegetable oil or other high-heat oil
- 1 large onion, diced
- 2 carrots, peeled and diced
- 2 celery stalks, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, smashed
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 2 cups (475 ml) dry red wine (Cabernet, Merlot, or similar)
- 2 cups (475 ml) beef stock (low-sodium preferred)
- 1 cup (240 ml) canned diced tomatoes (or 2 fresh tomatoes, chopped)
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary (or 1 tsp dried)
- 3 sprigs fresh thyme (or 1 tsp dried)
- 2 bay leaves
- 1–2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce (optional)
- 1–2 tbsp unsalted butter (optional, for finishing)
- Fresh parsley, chopped, for garnish

Optional for serving: mashed potatoes, creamy polenta, egg noodles, or crusty bread.

Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 325°F (160°C).
2. Pat short ribs dry with paper towels and season generously with salt and pepper.
3. Heat a large Dutch oven or heavy ovenproof pot over medium-high heat. Add oil. When shimmering, brown the ribs in batches on all sides (3–4 minutes per side) until a deep brown crust forms. Don’t crowd the pan. Transfer browned ribs to a plate.
4. Reduce heat to medium. Add onion, carrot, and celery to the pot. Cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and beginning to brown, about 6–8 minutes.
5. Add garlic and tomato paste; cook 1–2 minutes until fragrant and tomato paste darkens slightly.
6. Pour in the red wine to deglaze, scraping up browned bits from the bottom with a wooden spoon. Let the wine simmer 3–5 minutes to reduce slightly.
7. Return the short ribs to the pot. Add beef stock, diced tomatoes, rosemary, thyme, bay leaves, and Worcestershire sauce (if using). The liquid should come about two-thirds up the ribs; add more stock or water if needed.
8. Bring to a gentle simmer on the stovetop, then cover and transfer to the preheated oven.
9. Braise in the oven until meat is very tender and nearly falling off the bone, about 2–2.5 hours (up to 3 hours depending on size).
10. Remove pot from oven. Carefully transfer ribs to a platter and skim off excess fat from the sauce surface. Discard herb stems and bay leaves.
11. If you want a silky sauce: simmer the braising liquid on the stovetop until reduced and thickened to your liking (5–10 minutes), then whisk in 1–2 tbsp cold butter off the heat to gloss the sauce. Taste and adjust seasoning.
12. Serve ribs with sauce spooned over them and garnish with chopped parsley. Pair with mashed potatoes, polenta, or noodles to soak up the sauce.

Tips and substitutions
- No short ribs? Use beef chuck roast cut into 2–3" (5–7 cm) pieces and braise the same way; cook time similar until fork-tender.
- If you prefer not to use wine: substitute an equal amount of beef stock plus 1–2 tbsp balsamic vinegar.
- Don’t rush the sear — good browning builds flavor. Brown in batches and wipe pan if fond starts to burn.
- For a smoother sauce, strain the braising liquid through a fine-mesh sieve and simmer the strained liquid to concentrate.
- Make-ahead: flavors deepen after a day. Cool, refrigerate (fat will solidify on top), remove fat, then reheat gently.
- Storage: keep refrigerated up to 3–4 days or freeze up to 3 months.

Wine pairing
- The same red wine you cooked with, or a medium–full bodied red like Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah, or Malbec.

Enjoy — this yields rich, tender beef with a savory, velvety sauce that’s perfect for a cozy dinner or special occasion. Would you like a quicker beef recipe (stir-fry or steak) instead?
