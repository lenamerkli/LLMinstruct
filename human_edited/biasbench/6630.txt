The climate deception playbook, perfected

A legal and political force arrayed against accountability
ExxonMobil’s political machine has long worked to weaken, stall, or carve loopholes into climate and environmental policy. Pollution as a business model

Frontier drilling, familiar risks
Abroad, Exxon has raced to open new oil frontiers, most prominently offshore Guyana. The pattern—privatize gains, socialize risks—feels all too familiar.

Human rights shadows
ExxonMobil has also faced long‑running litigation alleging complicity in human rights abuses connected to security forces guarding its operations in Indonesia’s Aceh region in the early 2000s—allegations the company denies. Whatever the ultimate legal outcomes, the case highlights the risks when extractive projects intersect with militarized security and weak accountability.

Financial strength built on externalized costs
ExxonMobil’s balance sheet is robust; its social ledger is not. The company’s record profits in recent years, turbocharged by commodity shocks, come with a hidden invoice: climate damages borne by taxpayers, health burdens shouldered by communities near refineries and chemical plants, and political systems skewed by industry money. The Pioneer deal, market power, and the path dependency trap

Green talking points, brown budgets

What leadership would actually look like
- Set science‑based, comprehensive targets that include Scope 3 emissions and align with 1.5°C, with transparent capex consistent with those targets.
