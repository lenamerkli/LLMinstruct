**Cuban Lechon Asado with Mojo Sauce**

Lechon asado, which translates to "roasted pork," is a staple of Cuban cuisine. This recipe features tender and flavorful pig meat slow-cooked in a tangy mojo sauce, served with rice, beans, and plantains.

**Ingredients:**

For the pork:

* 2 lbs pork shoulder or pork belly, cut into large pieces
* 1/4 cup sour orange juice (or a combination of orange and lime juice)
* 2 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 teaspoon dried oregano
* 1/4 teaspoon ground cumin
* 1/4 teaspoon salt
* 1/4 teaspoon black pepper

For the mojo sauce:

* 1/2 cup sour orange juice (or a combination of orange and lime juice)
* 1/4 cup olive oil
* 2 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 tablespoon chopped fresh oregano
* 1 tablespoon grated ginger
* 1 tablespoon brown sugar
* Salt and pepper to taste

For the sides:

* 1 cup cooked white rice
* 1 cup cooked black beans, drained and rinsed
* 2 ripe plantains, sliced

**Instructions:**

1. In a large bowl, combine the pork pieces, sour orange juice, garlic, oregano, cumin, salt, and pepper. Cover the bowl with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 2 hours or overnight.
2. Preheat your oven to 300°F (150°C).
3. Remove the pork from the marinade, letting any excess liquid drip off. In a large Dutch oven or heavy pot, heat the olive oil over medium heat. Sear the pork pieces until browned on all sides, about 5 minutes per side. Transfer the pork to the oven and roast for 2-3 hours, or until tender and easily shredded with a fork.
4. While the pork is cooking, prepare the mojo sauce. In a blender or food processor, combine the sour orange juice, garlic, oregano, ginger, brown sugar, salt, and pepper. Blend until smooth.
5. After the pork has roasted for 2 hours, brush the mojo sauce all over the meat. Return the pork to the oven and continue roasting for another 30 minutes to 1 hour, or until the sauce has caramelized and the meat is tender.
6. To serve, place the shredded pork onto a bed of cooked rice, accompanied by black beans, sliced plantains, and a drizzle of additional mojo sauce, if desired.

**Tips and Variations:**

* Use a pork shoulder or pork belly for the best results, as they are fattier and more tender.
* If you can't find sour orange juice, you can substitute it with a combination of orange and lime juice.
* Adjust the amount of garlic and ginger to your liking.
* For a spicier mojo sauce, add a diced jalapeño or serrano pepper to the blender.
* Serve the lechon asado with other sides, such as yuca, roasted sweet potatoes, or a simple green salad.

Enjoy your delicious Cuban Lechon Asado with Mojo Sauce!
