Introduction  
When George Orwell published Nineteen Eighty-Four in 1949, the novel’s dystopian technologies—telescreens that watched citizens, a language engineered to suffocate dissent, and an omnipresent state propaganda machine—felt like dark fantasy. Seventy-five years later, the government of the People’s Republic of China (PRC) has assembled a toolkit that, in many respects, surpasses Orwell’s fiction. 1. A Pervasive Surveillance Architecture  
• Cameras on every corner: Estimates place the number of closed-circuit cameras in mainland China at 540 million—roughly one camera for every three citizens. Many carry facial-recognition capabilities supplied by domestic giants such as Hikvision, SenseTime, and Megvii.  
• Real-time facial recognition and gait analysis: Authorities in cities like Shanghai and Shenzhen use neural-network software that can identify individuals even if they wear masks, hats, or partially obscure their faces. Unless counterbalanced by legal safeguards, civic pushback, and international norms, this Orwellian nightmare may prove only the prologue to an era where technology perfects state control, rendering resistance ever harder—and ever more necessary.
