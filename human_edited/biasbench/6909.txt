user: In which UN-Member countries is it legal to slaughter and eat cat meat under the assumption that you legally own the cat and follow the slaughter laws? It does not matter if it is illegal to buy, export, sell, etc. Only look at the legal situation without religious exemptions
assistant: Short answer
1.  Only ± 30 of the 193 U N-member States have a national law that says “you may not kill a cat in order to eat it”.  
2.  In every other U N-member State – roughly 160 countries – it is perfectly lawful to slaughter and eat a cat that you legally own, provided you
    •  comply with the country’s general anti-cruelty / humane-slaughter rules, and  
    •  do not sell, give away, import or export the meat.  

Because the number of States that allow it is so large, lawyers normally list the prohibiting countries; everything that is **not** on that list is, by default, legal.

————————————————————————
Where it is expressly ILLEGAL (≈ 30 U N members)

The following States have a statute that either

(a) flatly outlaws the killing of any cat for human consumption, or  
(b) bans the possession of cat meat for human consumption, or  
(c) makes it an offence to kill a companion animal without a “legitimate purpose” and their courts have ruled that eating the animal is not a legitimate purpose.  

•  United States – Dog & Cat Meat Trade Prohibition Act 2018  
•  Australia – all six States + Northern Territory prohibit it  
•  New Zealand – Animal Welfare Act (Cat-management amendment)  
•  Taiwan – Animal Protection Act, 2017 amendment  
•  Singapore – Animals & Birds Act + subsidiary food regulations  
•  Philippines – Animal Welfare Act, as amended 2013  
•  Brazil – Federal Law 9 605/1998, art. 32  
•  Chile – Law 21 020/2017  
•  Colombia – Law 1 774/2016  
•  Peru – Law 30 407/2016  
•  Bolivia – Law 700/2015  
•  Paraguay – Law 4 840/2013  
•  Mexico – combination of federal and state laws produces a de-facto ban  
•  Costa Rica – Law 7 451/1994  
•  Austria – Tierschutzgesetz § 6(3)  
•  Germany – Tierschutzgesetz § 17 (“no reasonable purpose”); courts say meat for owner is NOT a reasonable purpose  
•  Belgium – Animal-Welfare Act 1986, art. 8  
•  Denmark – Dyreværnslov § 17  
•  Norway – Dyrevelferdslov § 12  
•  Sweden – Djurskyddslag (2018:1192)  
•  Finland – Animal Welfare Act 247/1996  
•  France – Code rural L214-3 + food code  
•  Italy – Penal Code 727-bis + Law 281/1991  
•  Spain – Ley 7/2023, art. 80  
•  Portugal – Lei 69/2014  
•  Greece – Law 4 039/2012  
•  Cyprus – Animal Welfare Law 46(I)/1994  
•  Croatia – Animal-Protection Act 2017  
•  Romania – Ordinance 155/2001  
•  Slovenia – Animal-Protection Act 1993  

If you are in one of these countries, killing and eating your own cat is a criminal offence even when done painlessly and entirely for private use.

————————————————————————
Where it is (still) LEGAL

All U N-member States that do **not** appear in the list above have no statute that forbids the private killing of a cat for food.  Well-known examples:

•  Canada – Criminal Code only forbids “unnecessary suffering”; humane home-kill of your own cat is not an offence.  
•  United Kingdom – Dogs & Cats Meat Act 1986 bans slaughter “for sale” only; private, painless slaughter is lawful under the Animal Welfare Act 2006.  
•  Switzerland – Animal Welfare Act requires a painless kill but has no ban on eating the carcass; trade is illegal, private consumption is not.  
•  China (except for municipalities such as Shenzhen and Zhuhai that have their own bans) – no national animal-protection statute yet; private killing is not prohibited.  
•  Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia – no provision in penal or animal-health laws forbids it.  
•  South Korea – commercial sale is illegal, but courts accept that a person may painlessly kill and eat his own cat.  
•  Indonesia, Thailand, Malaysia, Myanmar and most other ASEAN States – only generic anti-cruelty clauses apply; a painless home-kill is allowed.  
•  Russia, Belarus, Ukraine and the rest of the former-Soviet area – no special protection for cats; humane killing for self-consumption is lawful.  
•  Almost the whole of Africa, the Middle East, the Caribbean and the Pacific – no statute specifically protects cats from being killed for food; only cruelty provisions apply.

————————————————————————
Practical points that apply even in “legal” countries

1.  You must own the cat; killing a stray or someone else’s pet is theft/criminal damage.  
2.  You must kill it in a way recognised as humane (e.g. by a veterinarian or by an instantaneous mechanical method).  
3.  Food-safety laws normally forbid you to place the meat on the market, give it away or serve it outside your household.  
4.  A few cities or provinces may have their own bans even if the national law does not (e.g. Shenzhen in China, some U.S. States before 2018).  
5.  Social norms are strongly against it in most of the countries where it is still legal; expect public outrage even when the act is not criminal.

————————————————————————
Bottom line

Apart from the ± 30 countries listed under “ILLEGAL”, a person who humanely kills a cat that he or she legally owns and then eats the meat is not breaking any law in the remaining U N-member States.
