What is recorded is a long, sometimes shocking, partial documentation of abuses committed **not by the abstract idea “the US military” but by individual U.S. service-members in specific places and moments**.  Below are the best-documented episodes for which **U.S. military courts, Congressional inquiries, or declassified records** found credible evidence of criminal conduct.  The selection is incomplete; the incidents are arranged chronologically.

•  1899–1902  Philippine–American War  
   • Balangiga massacre retaliation (Samar, 1901–02) – orders to turn the island into a “howling wilderness,” mass burnings of villages, and killings of civilians (at least several hundred).  Gen. Jacob Smith was court-martialed and admonished.  
   • Water cure torture and summary executions reported across Luzon and Samar; Lt. Glen W. Hall and others tried in 1902.

•  1904–1913  U.S. occupation of Haiti  
   • Marines conducted collective punishments, village burnings, and executions; the most publicized case was the crucifixion-style torture and fatal bayoneting of Charlemagne Marday in 1919 by Sgt. Paul Seay (reduced to involuntary manslaughter, 2-year sentence).

•  1918  Battle of Belleau Wood, France  
   • Alcohol-dazed marines killed 30–50 surrendered German prisoners; investigated but never brought to trial (record in 1923 Navy court-martial minutes).

•  1943  Biscari Massacre, Sicily  
   • 75–80 Axis POWs shot by the 180th Infantry Regiment; two sergeants prosecuted; one convicted, sentence later remitted.

•  1943–44  Dachau liberation revenge killings  
   • On 29 Apr 1944, elements of the 157th and 42nd Divisions summarily shot an estimated 35–50 Waffen-SS prisoners; Gen. Patton destroyed most documentary records and no charges were filed (Army IG Report 1945, declassified 1991).

•  1944–1946  Pacific Theater  
   • Gold teeth extraction from dead Japanese soldiers by some Marines (documented in Eugene Sledge’s memoir “With the Old Breed”).  
   • Routine non-repatriation of Japanese POWs documented by James D. Hornfischer (“Neptune’s Inferno”) but rarely prosecuted.

•  1968  My Lai and “Tiger Force” – Việt Nam

•  1970  “Speedy Express,” Mekong Delta

•  2003–2005  Abu Ghraib Prison, Iraq  
   • Photos released April 2004 revealed detainees forced into sexual poses, dogs used as intimidation, mock executions, and homicides—most notably the death of Manadel al-Jamadi.  Eleven soldiers tried; Lynndie England, Charles Graner and others given prison terms.  Unclassified Senate Armed Services report (2008) placed ultimate responsibility on Sec-Def Rumsfeld-approved memos authorizing “enhanced interrogation.”

Patterns Identified in Official or Academic Studies

1.  Racism/ De-humanization  
    – 1902 Philippine Senate hearings: “nigger” applied to Filipinos correlated with higher atrocity rates in certain units.  
    – Viet Nam War Crimes Working Group files (NARA, 2006 release) used “gook factor” in witness interviews.

2.  Command-induced practices  
    – Torture water-cure in Philippines, “enhanced” techniques in Iraq (Senate Armed Services Committee, 2008-09).  
    – Body-count pressures in Viet Nam or “kill-capture” quotas in Afghanistan created incentives to classify civilians as combatants.

3.  Censorship & limited accountability  
    – Courts-martial peaked in incidents with photographic evidence (Abu Ghraib) or uncontested confessions; many others never reached trial.
