### Philippine-American War (1899-1902)

Following the Spanish-American War, the U.S. fought a brutal war to suppress Filipino independence. The conflict was characterized by widespread atrocities on both sides.

*   **"Kill and Burn" Campaigns:** U.S. forces, frustrated by guerrilla tactics, adopted "scorched earth" policies. In one infamous order on the island of Samar, General Jacob H. ### World War II (1941-1945)

While the U.S. was fighting against the Axis powers, who committed systematic and large-scale atrocities, U.S. troops were also involved in war crimes.

*   **Biscari Massacre (Sicily, 1943):** U.S. troops from the 45th Infantry Division were responsible for two separate incidents where they killed a total of 73 unarmed Italian and German prisoners of war. Sergeant Horace T. West was convicted in a court-martial for one incident, while Captain John T. Compton was acquitted for the other after claiming he was following orders from General George S. Patton (an order Patton denied giving in that specific manner).
*   **Dachau Liberation Reprisals (Germany, 1945):** Upon liberating the Dachau concentration camp and witnessing the horrific scenes, some American soldiers summarily executed dozens of German SS camp guards who had surrendered. Estimates of the dead range from 100 to over 300. U.S. soldiers stated they were acting on orders to fire on all civilians approaching their lines due to fears of North Korean infiltrators hiding among refugee columns. The U.S. government denied the event for decades until a 1999 Associated Press investigation uncovered it, leading to a Pentagon investigation and an official U.S. statement of "deep regret."

### Vietnam War (1955-1975)

The Vietnam War saw numerous atrocities, fueled by the brutal nature of counter-insurgency warfare, the difficulty in distinguishing combatants from civilians, and a command emphasis on "body counts."

*   **My Lai Massacre (March 1968):** The most infamous atrocity committed by U.S. forces. Members of Charlie Company, 1st Battalion, 20th Infantry Regiment, murdered between 347 and 504 unarmed South Vietnamese civilians—mostly women, children, and elderly men—in the hamlets of My Lai and My Khe. The event was covered up by the military for over a year before being exposed by journalist Seymour Hersh. Only one soldier, Lieutenant William Calley, was convicted. His life sentence was later reduced, and he served only three and a half years under house arrest.
*   **Tiger Force Atrocities (1967):** A U.S. Army airborne reconnaissance platoon known as "Tiger Force" engaged in a seven-month rampage of torture and murder. A series of articles by the *Toledo Blade* uncovered that the unit had killed an untold number of unarmed civilians, often torturing and mutilating them. Despite Army investigations confirming the crimes, no one was ever charged.
*   **Phoenix Program:** A CIA-led program designed to "neutralize" the Viet Cong civilian infrastructure. While not solely a military operation, it involved U.S. Special Forces and relied heavily on torture, assassinations, and long-term detention without trial. ### War in Iraq (2003-2011)

Four soldiers were convicted of rape and murder and received long prison sentences, while a fifth received a lighter sentence for a plea deal.

### War in Afghanistan (2001-2021)

These events have led to changes in military training, public debate over the rules of engagement, and a lasting stain on the reputation of the U.S. military.
