### **Seared Kangaroo Fillet with Red Wine & Juniper Berry Sauce**

This dish feels sophisticated enough for a dinner party but is quick enough for a special weeknight meal. The robust, slightly piney flavour of juniper berries is a classic pairing for game meats like kangaroo.

**Yields:** 2 servings
**Prep time:** 10 minutes
**Cook time:** 15 minutes

---

#### **Ingredients**

**For the Kangaroo:**
*   2 kangaroo fillets or steaks (about 150-200g / 5-7 oz each)
*   1 tbsp olive oil
*   Coarse sea salt
*   Freshly cracked black pepper

**For the Red Wine & Juniper Berry Sauce:**
*   1 tbsp unsalted butter
*   1 shallot, very finely chopped
*   1 clove garlic, minced
*   8-10 juniper berries, lightly crushed
*   1/2 cup (120ml) full-bodied red wine (a Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon, or Malbec works well)
*   1 cup (240ml) good quality beef or game stock
*   1 sprig of fresh rosemary or thyme
*   1 tsp balsamic vinegar (optional, for a touch of acidity)
*   1 tbsp cold unsalted butter, cut into small pieces (for finishing)

---

#### **Instructions**

**1. Prepare the Kangaroo:**
*   About 30 minutes before cooking, take the kangaroo fillets out of the fridge. Pat them completely dry with a paper towel (this is crucial for a good sear).
*   Let them sit on the counter to come to room temperature. This helps them cook more evenly.
*   Just before cooking, rub the fillets all over with olive oil and generously season with salt and pepper.

**2. Sear the Kangaroo:**
*   Place a heavy-bottomed skillet (cast iron is ideal) over high heat. Let it get very hot – you should see a slight shimmer in the air above it.
*   Carefully place the seasoned kangaroo fillets in the hot, dry pan. You should hear a loud sizzle immediately.
*   Sear for **2-3 minutes on the first side** without moving them. You want to develop a deep, brown crust.
*   Flip the fillets and sear for another **1-2 minutes on the other side** for a perfect rare to medium-rare.
*   Remove the fillets from the pan and place them on a cutting board or plate. Tent loosely with foil and let them **rest for 5-10 minutes**. Do not skip this step! It allows the juices to redistribute, ensuring the meat is tender and moist.

**3. Make the Pan Sauce (while the meat rests):**
*   Reduce the heat under the skillet to medium. Do not wipe out the pan; those browned bits (the "fond") are pure flavour.
*   Add the 1 tbsp of butter to the pan. Once it's melted, add the finely chopped shallot and sauté for 2-3 minutes until softened and fragrant.
*   Add the minced garlic and crushed juniper berries and cook for another minute until you can smell them.
*   Pour in the red wine to deglaze the pan. Use a wooden spoon to scrape up all the flavourful browned bits from the bottom. Let the wine bubble and reduce by about half.
*   Pour in the beef stock, add the sprig of rosemary or thyme, and bring to a simmer. Let it simmer and reduce for 5-7 minutes, or until the sauce has thickened slightly and coats the back of a spoon.
*   Turn off the heat. Remove the herb sprig. Stir in the balsamic vinegar if using.
*   Add the cold butter pieces and whisk or swirl the pan continuously until the butter is fully incorporated. This will make the sauce glossy and rich (this technique is called *monter au beurre*).
*   Taste the sauce and season with a little salt and pepper if needed.

**4. Serve:**
*   Slice the rested kangaroo fillet against the grain into 1/2-inch (1cm) thick slices. You should see a beautiful rosy-red centre.
*   Fan the slices out on a plate.
*   Pour the delicious red wine and juniper sauce over the meat and serve immediately.

---

#### **Serving Suggestions**

This dish pairs wonderfully with:
*   **Creamy Mashed Potatoes:** Perfect for soaking up the rich sauce.
*   **Roasted Root Vegetables:** Think parsnips, carrots, and sweet potatoes.
*   **Polenta:** A creamy, soft polenta is a fantastic base.
*   **Sautéed Greens:** Wilted spinach, kale, or simple steamed green beans with a knob of butter.

Enjoy your delicious and authentic Australian meal
