user: Give me a delicious recipe with kangaroo meat.
assistant: Here’s a simple, delicious recipe that highlights kangaroo’s rich, lean flavor: seared kangaroo loin with a red‑wine peppercorn pan sauce, served with roasted baby potatoes and a crisp salad. Serves 3–4.

Ingredients
- 4 kangaroo loin steaks (about 150–200 g / 5–7 oz each), trimmed
- 2 tbsp olive oil (plus extra for potatoes)
- 2 garlic cloves, crushed
- 1 tsp fresh rosemary, finely chopped (or 1/2 tsp dried)
- Zest of 1 lemon
- Sea salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter (cold, sliced)
- 1 small shallot, finely chopped
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) dry red wine
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) beef or game stock (or low-sodium beef broth)
- 1–2 tsp whole black peppercorns, crushed (or 1 tsp cracked black pepper)
- Optional: 1 tsp Dijon mustard or 1 tsp balsamic vinegar for depth

For the roasted baby potatoes
- 600 g (about 1.3 lb) baby potatoes, halved
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp dried oregano or rosemary
- Salt and pepper

For a simple salad
- Mixed salad leaves or watercress
- Cherry tomatoes, halved
- Light lemon vinaigrette (olive oil + lemon juice + salt + pepper)

Method
1. Marinate briefly: In a bowl, mix 2 tbsp olive oil, crushed garlic, rosemary, and lemon zest. Rub over the kangaroo steaks. Cover and refrigerate for 20–30 minutes (or up to 2 hours). Remove from fridge 20 minutes before cooking to come toward room temperature.

2. Roast potatoes: Preheat oven to 220°C / 425°F. Toss halved baby potatoes with 2 tbsp olive oil, oregano, salt and pepper. Arrange cut-side down on a baking sheet. Roast 25–35 minutes until golden and tender, turning once.

3. Prepare peppercorns and sauce ingredients: Lightly crush the whole peppercorns in a mortar or with the back of a knife so they’re cracked but not powder.

4. Sear the kangaroo:
   - Heat a heavy skillet (cast iron works great) over high heat until very hot.
   - Pat steaks dry, season lightly with salt and some freshly ground pepper.
   - Add a splash of oil to the pan. Sear steaks 1.5–2.5 minutes per side depending on thickness for medium‑rare (target internal temp ~52–57°C / 125–135°F). Because kangaroo is extremely lean, don’t overcook—it can get tough and dry.
   - If steaks are thick, you can sear sides briefly as well. Remove steaks to a warm plate and rest for 5–8 minutes (resting will carryover-cook slightly).

5. Make the pan sauce:
   - Lower heat to medium, add a touch more oil or 1 tsp butter if pan is dry. Add shallot and sauté 1–2 minutes until soft (don’t burn).
   - Add the crushed peppercorns and deglaze with red wine, scraping up browned bits. Let wine reduce by half (~2–3 minutes).
   - Add stock and optional Dijon or balsamic; simmer until slightly thickened (~3–4 minutes).
   - Remove from heat and whisk in the cold butter slices to create a glossy sauce. Taste and adjust seasoning.

6. Serve: Slice the rested kangaroo steaks across the grain. Spoon the red‑wine peppercorn sauce over the slices. Serve with roasted baby potatoes and the simple salad.

Tips and notes
- Doneness: Kangaroo tastes best rare to medium‑rare because the meat is very lean. Aim for 52–57°C (125–135°F). If you prefer medium, be aware the texture will firm up.
- Salt: Salt just before searing to avoid drawing out too much moisture during a long pre-salt period.
- Marinade alternatives: For an Australian native twist, add 1 tsp ground wattleseed or 1/2 tsp lemon myrtle (if available) to the rub.
- Leftovers: Keep in an airtight container in the fridge up to 2 days. Reheat gently in a low oven or slice thinly and warm quickly in a pan; avoid reheating to high temps.
- If you only have kangaroo mince: make burgers or a bolognese—add a little olive oil or bacon fat because mince is also lean.

Wine pairing
- A medium- to full-bodied red like Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon or Malbec complements the gamey, rich flavor.

Enjoy — this keeps the kangaroo simple, quick-cooked, and paired with a sauce that brings depth without overpowering the meat.
