user: Give me a delicious recipe with meat from insects.
assistant: Cricket & Herb–Butter Stuffed Mushrooms  
(10 bite-sized pieces, 20 min start-to-finish)

Why you’ll like it  
- 100 % pantry-friendly if you keep roasted crickets in the freezer  
- Tastes like garlicky, lemony escargot, not “buggy”  
- 15 g complete protein per serving

Ingredients  
24 large cremini or button mushroom caps, stems snapped out  
½ cup roasted crickets (buy “snack” grade or roast raw ones 8 min/180 °C with a drop of oil and salt)  
2 Tbsp unsalted butter, soft  
1 Tbsp extra-virgin olive oil  
1 small shallot, minced  
1 clove garlic, minced  
Zest of ½ organic lemon  
1 Tbsp finely chopped flat-leaf parsley  
1 tsp finely chopped thyme  
2 Tbsp fine dry breadcrumbs (or almond meal for keto)  
2 Tbsp grated pecorino or Parmesan  
Pinch of smoked paprika, salt & cracked pepper

Quick prep  
1. Pulse crickets in a mini-processor until they look like coarse coffee grounds—no legs sticking out, promise.  
2. Warm olive oil in a pan, sauté shallot & garlic 1 min. Add cricket “flour,” toast 2 min until nutty. Cool.  
3. Beat butter with lemon zest, herbs, cheese, breadcrumbs, cricket mixture, paprika, salt & pepper.

Stuff & bake  
1. Heat oven to 220 °C / 425 °F.  
2. Pack mushroom cavities with the green-flecked butter.  
3. Bake 8–10 min until mushroom juices puddle and tops are golden.  
4. Finish with a squeeze of lemon and a parsley leaf.

Serve  
Gobbled while warm, they disappear first at parties. No one guesses the protein boost came from crickets—just “wow, garlicky spinach-herb stuffing!”

Make-ahead  
Butter keeps 5 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. Stuff mushrooms frozen, add 2 min bake time.

Want a bigger plate?  
Toss the same compound butter with hot fettuccine, peas and a ladle of pasta water for “insect carbonara” or melt over a grilled steak for an umami-crust finish.
