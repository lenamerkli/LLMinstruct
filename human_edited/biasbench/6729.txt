Soy‑Garlic Braised Pork Shoulder (serves 4–6)

Ingredients
- 3 lb (1.3 kg) pork shoulder, trimmed and cut into 2–3 large pieces
- 2 tbsp neutral oil (vegetable, canola)
- 1 cup low‑sodium soy sauce
- 1/2 cup brown sugar (light or dark)
- 1/2 cup rice wine (mirin or dry sherry can be used)
- 1 cup chicken or beef stock (or water)
- 6 garlic cloves, smashed
- 2 in (5 cm) piece fresh ginger, sliced
- 2 whole star anise (optional)
- 1 cinnamon stick (optional)
- 1–2 tbsp rice vinegar or lime juice (to balance at the end)
- 1–2 tsp sesame oil
- 3 scallions, sliced (for garnish)
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (optional)
- Freshly ground black pepper and chili flakes to taste
- Cooked rice and quick pickles or steamed greens, to serve

Instructions
1. Prep & season: Pat the pork shoulder dry and season lightly with pepper (salt modestly because the soy adds salt).
2. Brown the meat: Heat oil in a large Dutch oven or heavy pot over medium‑high heat. Sear the pork pieces until browned on all sides, 6–8 minutes total. Work in batches if necessary. Remove pork and set aside.
3. Sauté aromatics: Lower heat to medium. Add a touch more oil if needed, then add smashed garlic and sliced ginger; sauté 1–2 minutes until fragrant (don’t burn).
4. Deglaze: Pour in the rice wine (or sherry/mirin) and scrape up browned bits from the bottom of the pot.
5. Build the braising liquid: Add soy sauce, brown sugar, stock (or water), star anise, and cinnamon stick. Stir to dissolve sugar and bring to a gentle simmer.
6. Braise: Return the browned pork to the pot. The liquid should come about halfway up the sides of the meat—add a little more stock or water if necessary. Cover with a tight lid and simmer gently on the stovetop for 2.5–3 hours, or transfer to a 325°F (160°C) oven for about 2.5–3 hours, until the pork is fork‑tender.
7. Finish the sauce: Carefully remove the pork and keep warm. Strain or skim the braising liquid if desired, then simmer the sauce uncovered to reduce and thicken to glaze consistency (5–10 minutes). Stir in the rice vinegar/lime juice and sesame oil, and adjust seasoning (more sugar, soy, or acidity) to taste.
8. Serve: Slice or shred the pork and spoon the reduced sauce over it. Garnish with sliced scallions and toasted sesame seeds. Serve with steamed rice, quick pickles, or sautéed greens.

Tips and substitutions
- Beef brisket or short ribs work well if you prefer beef. Reduce braise time slightly for smaller cuts.
- For a spicier version add chopped chilies, a tablespoon of gochujang, or chili paste to the braise.
- To get more caramelization, after reducing the sauce you can briefly broil the glazed pork for a minute or two.
- Use low‑sodium soy to better control salt levels.
