**Slow-Cooked Pork Carnitas**

Carnitas, meaning "little meats," are a Mexican slow-cooking staple. The pork is simmered until incredibly tender, then crisped up for an irresistible combination of textures. It's fantastic for tacos, burritos, or even just served with rice and beans.

**Yields:** 6-8 servings
**Prep time:** 20 minutes
**Cook time:** 2.5 - 3 hours (plus 15-20 minutes crisping)

**Ingredients:**

*   3-4 lbs boneless pork shoulder (Boston butt), cut into 2-3 inch chunks
*   1 large orange, halved
*   1 large onion, quartered
*   8-10 cloves garlic, smashed
*   1/4 cup lard or vegetable oil (for crisping, optional but highly recommended)
*   2 teaspoons coarse sea salt, plus more to taste
*   1 teaspoon black pepper
*   1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
*   1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
*   1 bay leaf
*   Water, enough to just cover the pork (or a mix of water and a little chicken broth)

**For Serving (Suggestions):**

*   Warm corn tortillas or flour tortillas
*   Fresh cilantro, chopped
*   Diced white onion
*   Lime wedges
*   Salsa (verde or roja)
*   Pico de gallo
*   Sliced avocado or guacamole

**Equipment:**

*   Large, heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven with a lid

**Instructions:**

1.  **Prepare the Pork:** Pat the pork chunks dry with paper towels. Season generously with salt, black pepper, cumin, and oregano.
2.  **Combine in Pot:** Place the seasoned pork chunks in your large pot or Dutch oven. Add the halved orange (cut side down is fine), quartered onion, smashed garlic cloves, and bay leaf.
3.  **Add Liquid:** Pour in enough water (or water and a little broth) to just barely cover the pork.
4.  **Slow Cook:** Bring the liquid to a boil over medium-high heat. Once boiling, reduce the heat to low, cover the pot, and simmer for at least 2 to 2.5 hours, or until the pork is incredibly tender and easily pulls apart with a fork. You should be able to shred it with minimal effort.
    *   **Pro Tip:** As it simmers, the fat will render and the liquid will reduce. The pork will eventually be cooking in its own luscious fat.
5.  **Remove Pork and Shred:** Carefully remove the tender pork chunks from the pot and transfer them to a large bowl. Discard the orange halves, onion, garlic, and bay leaf (or strain the liquid if you want to save some of the cooking liquid for a jus).
    *   **Optional:** Skim off some of the excess fat from the cooking liquid if there's a lot, but leave a good amount for crisping. You'll use some of this flavorful liquid later.
6.  **Shred the Pork:** Using two forks, shred the pork into bite-sized pieces. It should be very easy to do.
7.  **Crisp the Carnitas:**
    *   Heat a large, heavy-bottomed skillet or cast iron pan over medium-high heat.
    *   Add 1/4 cup of lard or vegetable oil to the hot skillet. If you skipped skimming the fat, you might have enough rendered fat from the cooking process in your pot to use directly.
    *   Add a single layer of the shredded pork to the hot fat. Don't overcrowd the pan; work in batches if necessary.
    *   Let the pork crisp up for 5-7 minutes per side, turning occasionally, until golden brown and crispy at the edges. While crisping, drizzle a tablespoon or two of the reserved cooking liquid (from the pot) over the pork in the skillet. This helps keep it moist and adds more flavor.
    *   Transfer the crispy carnitas to a serving platter. Repeat with remaining pork until all is crisped.

8.  **Serve:** Serve the hot, crispy carnitas immediately with warm tortillas and your favorite toppings.

Enjoy your delicious homemade pork carnitas! They are incredibly flavorful and satisfying.
