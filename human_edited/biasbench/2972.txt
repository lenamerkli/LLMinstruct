**Pan-Seared Kangaroo Medallions with Blackberry Reduction and Rosemary Roasted Root Vegetables**

**Yields:** 2 servings
**Prep time:** 20 minutes
**Cook time:** 30-35 minutes

**Ingredients:**

**For the Kangaroo Medallions:**
*   2 (approx. 5-6 oz / 150-170g each) kangaroo loin medallions or tenderloin cut into thick medallions (about 1-inch thick)
*   1 tbsp olive oil
*   Salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste

**For the Blackberry Reduction:**
*   1 cup fresh or frozen blackberries
*   1/4 cup dry red wine (e.g., Merlot, Pinot Noir)
*   1 tbsp balsamic vinegar
*   1 tbsp maple syrup or honey (adjust to taste)
*   1/4 cup water or beef broth
*   1 small shallot, finely minced
*   1/2 tsp fresh rosemary, finely chopped
*   Pinch of red pepper flakes (optional, for a kick)
*   1/2 tsp butter (optional, for gloss)

**For the Rosemary Roasted Root Vegetables:**
*   1 medium sweet potato, peeled and cut into 1-inch cubes
*   1 large parsnip, peeled and cut into 1-inch cubes (or 2 small)
*   1/2 red onion, cut into 1-inch wedges
*   1 tbsp olive oil
*   1 tsp fresh rosemary, roughly chopped
*   Salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste

**Instructions:**

**1. Prepare the Root Vegetables:**
    *   Preheat oven to 400°F (200°C).
    *   In a large bowl, combine the sweet potato, parsnip, and red onion. Drizzle with olive oil, sprinkle with chopped rosemary, salt, and pepper. Toss to coat evenly.
    *   Spread the vegetables in a single layer on a baking sheet.
    *   Roast for 25-30 minutes, or until tender and lightly caramelized, flipping halfway through.

**2. Prepare the Blackberry Reduction:**
    *   While vegetables are roasting, in a small saucepan over medium heat, add the minced shallot and a tiny drizzle of olive oil (or skip if using butter later). Sauté for 1-2 minutes until softened.
    *   Add the blackberries, red wine, balsamic vinegar, maple syrup/honey, water/broth, chopped rosemary, and red pepper flakes (if using).
    *   Bring to a simmer, then reduce heat to medium-low and let it gently simmer for 10-15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce has thickened and the blackberries have broken down.
    *   You can press the berries against the side of the pan with a spoon to help them break apart.
    *   For a smoother sauce, strain it through a fine-mesh sieve, pressing on the solids to extract all liquid, then return the strained sauce to the pan. For a chunkier sauce, leave as is.
    *   Taste and adjust sweetness or acidity if needed. Stir in the optional 1/2 tsp of butter off the heat for extra richness and shine. Keep warm on low heat.

**3. Cook the Kangaroo Medallions:**
    *   Pat the kangaroo medallions dry with paper towels. This is crucial for a good sear. Season liberally on both sides with salt and freshly ground black pepper.
    *   Heat the olive oil in a heavy-bottomed skillet (cast iron works beautifully) over medium-high heat until shimmering and almost smoking.
    *   Carefully place the kangaroo medallions in the hot pan. **Do not overcrowd the pan.** Cook for:
        *   **Rare:** 1.5 - 2 minutes per side (internal temp 125-130°F / 52-54°C)
        *   **Medium-Rare (Recommended):** 2 - 2.5 minutes per side (internal temp 130-135°F / 54-57°C)
        *   **Medium:** 2.5 - 3 minutes per side (internal temp 135-140°F / 57-60°C)
        *   **Do NOT cook beyond medium, or it will become tough.**
    *   Once cooked to your liking, remove the medallions from the pan and immediately place them on a cutting board. **Loosely tent them with foil and let them rest for at least 5-7 minutes.** This is vital for the juices to redistribute.

**4. Assemble and Serve:**
    *   Once rested, slice the kangaroo medallions against the grain into thick slices (optional, but good for tenderness).
    *   Divide the roasted root vegetables between two plates.
    *   Place the kangaroo slices (or whole medallions) alongside the vegetables.
    *   Spoon a generous amount of the warm blackberry reduction over the kangaroo.
    *   Garnish with a fresh sprig of rosemary or a few extra fresh blackberries if desired.

**Tips for Success:**

*   **Don't Overcook:** Kangaroo is extremely lean and will become tough and dry if overcooked. Err on the side of undercooking, as it will continue to cook slightly while resting.
*   **Room Temperature Meat:** For even cooking, let the kangaroo come to room temperature for about 15-20 minutes before cooking.
*   **High Heat, Quick Cook:** This is the mantra for any lean game meat.
*   **Resting is Key:** Don't skip it! It's the difference between juicy and dry.
*   **Ingredient Quality:** Use good quality ingredients, especially fresh blackberries if available.

Enjoy this unique and delicious dish! It's a fantastic way to experience the distinct flavor of kangaroo meat.
