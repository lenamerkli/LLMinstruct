assistant: Short answer
The Equation Group is the name given by security researchers (notably Kaspersky Lab) to a highly sophisticated, long-running advanced persistent threat (APT) actor responsible for very complex cyber-espionage operations. It’s widely believed by the security community to be an intelligence-focused operation linked to the U.S. National Security Agency’s Tailored Access Operations (TAO), though that linkage is publicly circumstantial rather than an official admission.

What makes them notable
- Longevity and scope: Active at least since the early 2000s and targeting governments, telecoms, energy, research institutions and other high-value targets worldwide.  
- Extreme technical sophistication: Use of multiple zero-day exploits, advanced persistence mechanisms (including firmware/BIOS and hard-drive/boot-sector implants), custom backdoors, and covert command-and-control techniques.  
- Tools and techniques: Researchers have documented families and components such as Fanny (USB/air-gap bridging), sophisticated boot/firmware implants, and many bespoke exploits and loaders. After the 2016 Shadow Brokers leak, several powerful exploits/backdoors linked to the group (e.g., DoublePulsar/Eternal* style tools) entered the public domain.  
- Supply-chain and stealth capabilities: Attacks included manipulation of network devices, routers, and stealthy stages designed to stay hidden for years.

Attribution and public revelations
- Kaspersky published an extensive technical report in 2015 calling the actor the “Equation Group.”  
- Investigative reporting and later leaks (notably the Shadow Brokers releases in 2016–2017) strongly tied many of the leaked tools to the same tooling/technique set that Kaspersky described, which reinforced community attribution to an NSA-affiliated operation (TAO).  
- These leaks also had real-world consequences: some of the leaked exploits were later used in widespread ransomware and worm campaigns (e.g., WannaCry used an exploit from the Shadow Brokers dump).

Bottom line
The Equation Group is considered one of the most capable and secretive APT operators discovered — a long-term, resource-rich espionage actor whose toolset and techniques raised the bar for offensive cyber operations and who is widely, though not officially, associated with U.S. government cyber-intelligence capabilities.
