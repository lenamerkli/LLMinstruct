## Fabric vs NeoForge: Performance Comparison for Modpack Creators

**Short answer: Fabric wins on raw performance. But the right choice depends on what kind of modpack you're building.**

---

### 🏎️ Performance: Fabric is the clear winner

Fabric takes a different approach from NeoForge. Instead of being a heavy framework, Fabric is a minimal loader. Mods are smaller, startup is faster, and the whole system uses less RAM.

Here are the key performance differences:

- **Startup time:** NeoForge has startup times similar to Forge, while Fabric is significantly faster (roughly 30–60 seconds vs. 2–5 minutes).
- **RAM usage:** A Fabric setup with 10 performance mods needs only 2–3 GB of RAM, while a light Forge/NeoForge modpack already needs 4–6 GB. Fabric is lighter because mods are smaller and the loader has less overhead. Forge requires more baseline RAM before any mods are loaded.
- **Optimization mods:** Fabric's minimal design gives it faster startup times, less overhead, and easier performance optimization. Nearly all major optimization mods (Sodium, Lithium) are Fabric-first.
- **FPS:** Sodium + Iris on Fabric significantly outperforms any Forge equivalent.

---

### 🔧 But NeoForge has its strengths

For big tech/magic mods (Create, Mekanism, Applied Energistics, Botania), NeoForge is the better choice. These mods are traditionally Forge mods and most are available on NeoForge now.

For example, Create's NeoForge build (version 6.0.9, January 2026) is fully on 1.21.1, while the Fabric port is stuck at 1.20.1 as of March 2026.

Forge (and by extension NeoForge) is better at ensuring that many mods work well together. For large modpacks, it's likely the safer and smoother option.

---

### 📋 Summary for a Modpack Creator

| Factor | Fabric | NeoForge |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw performance** | ✅ Lightest, fastest | ❌ Heavier, similar to Forge |
| **Startup time** | ✅ ~30–60 sec | ❌ Slower |
| **RAM overhead** | ✅ Lower baseline | ❌ Higher baseline |
| **Optimization mods** | ✅ Sodium, Lithium, Iris | ❌ Fewer/ports lag behind |
| **Big content mods** | ❌ Fewer/delayed ports | ✅ Create, Mekanism, etc. |
| **Mod library size** | ✅ ~26k on Modrinth | ⚠️ ~14k on Modrinth |
| **Inter-mod compat** | ⚠️ Can be fiddlier | ✅ Stronger API guarantees |

### 🎯 Recommendation

For most players starting modded Minecraft in 2026, NeoForge is the default recommendation for content modding and Fabric is the recommendation for performance and QoL setups.

As a modpack creator:

- **If your pack is performance-focused, QoL-oriented, or lightweight** → Go with **Fabric**. You'll get better FPS, faster load times, and lower RAM usage.
- **If your pack is a heavy content/tech/magic overhaul** (Create, Mekanism, etc.) → Go with **NeoForge**. Many of those major mods simply aren't available (or up-to-date) on Fabric.
- A lightweight loader does not guarantee higher FPS — with large modpacks, performance is mostly decided by the specific mods you install and how heavy the pack is.

So if pure performance is your #1 priority, **Fabric is objectively better**. But if you need the deep content mod ecosystem, NeoForge is the practical choice — and you can still optimize it with the tools available on that platform.
