### Translating “next” into Latin

The English word **“next”** has several uses, so its Latin translation depends on **context**. Below are the most common meanings and their appropriate Latin equivalents.

---

#### 1. **Next in time or order**
*(e.g., “next week”, “the next page”, “the next person”)*

**Latin:** **proxĭmus / sequēns**

* **proximus, -a, -um** – literally “nearest” or “closest,” and is the standard word for “next” in time/sequence.
  - *next week* → **proxima hebdomas**
  - *the next day* → **proximus dies**

* **sequēns, -entis** – “following,” often used for items in a sequence.
  - *the next chapter* → **capitulum sequens**

📌 **Most common choice for “next” (time/order): `proximus`**

---

#### 2. **Next to (spatial proximity)**
*(e.g., “the house next to the river”)*

**Latin:** **iuxtā** (preposition) or **proximus** (as an adjective)

* Using the preposition:
  - *next to the tree* → **iuxtā arborem**

* Using the adjective:
  - *the shop next to the school* → **taberna proxima scholae**

📌 **For “next to” → `iuxtā`**

---

#### 3. **The next one (among several)**
When you mean “the following one,” **sequēns** is often best.

> *Take the next bus.* → **Sume autobus sequentem.**

---

#### 4. **Adverbial use (“then”, “next”)**
In instructions or narratives (“first… next…”):

**Latin:** **deinde** or **postea**

> First we eat, next we dance. → **Primo edimus, deinde saltamus.**

---

### Quick Reference Table

| English Phrase       | Latin Translation                     |
|----------------------|---------------------------------------|
| next week            | **proxima hebdomas**                  |
| next day             | **proximus dies** / **cras** (only for “tomorrow”) |
| next page            | **proximum folium** / **pagina sequens** |
| next to (beside)     | **iuxtā** (e.g., *iuxtā mē* = next to me) |
| the next person      | **proxima persona** / **persona sequens** |
| next (in sequence)  | **sequēns**                           |
| next (then)          | **deinde** / **postea**               |

---

**⚠️ Important:**
Latin does **not** have a single word that covers every use of English “next.” Always choose the word based on context!

**Most versatile translation for “next” (time/sequence):** **`proximus`**.
