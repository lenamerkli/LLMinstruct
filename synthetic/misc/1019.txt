How much prison time did the Tobacco company CEOs get for lying under oath claiming that nicotine is not addictive?
§a§
**Zero.** None of the seven tobacco executives who testified before Congress on **April 14, 1994**, received prison time—or was convicted of perjury—for denying nicotine’s addictiveness.

The Justice Department investigated possible perjury charges but ultimately abandoned them. The executives had carefully framed their answers as personal beliefs—typically, “I believe nicotine is not addictive”—making it difficult to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that they knowingly lied about what they personally believed.

Major tobacco companies were later found liable in a **2006 civil RICO case** and ordered to issue corrective statements about nicotine addiction, but that civil judgment did not result in prison sentences for those CEOs.
