This meme captures a specific, bittersweet milestone in gender-affirming **Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT)**.

**The Setup**
For many transgender people, HRT induces a "second puberty," triggering physical changes like fat redistribution, breast growth, or muscle development. Stretch marks are a common side effect of rapid growth or skin expansion—particularly on the chest, hips, thighs, or shoulders.

**The Punchline**
The meme expresses a mix of **validation and melodramatic regret**:

*   **"I've won"** — The stretch mark is tangible proof that HRT is working and the body is changing in desired ways. It’s a milestone that confirms progress.
*   **"But at what cost?"** — A mock-tragic lament about the physical reality of that progress. Stretch marks are harmless but often stigmatized, so the wearer is jokingly mourning their "flawless" skin while celebrating the change that caused it.

**The Format**
Using a dramatic, low-resolution image of **Wario** (a Nintendo character known for being gross and over-the-top) adds to the humor. It frames a relatively minor physical change as an epic, almost Shakespearean sacrifice, which resonates with the intense emotional weight transition milestones often carry.

In short, it’s a self-deprecating, relatable joke about being **thrilled your body is changing** and **dramatically grieving the perfectly smooth skin you used to have**.

Where are these stretch marks on the body?
§a§
Stretch marks from HRT typically show up wherever the skin stretches rapidly due to fat redistribution, muscle growth, or breast development. The specific locations depend on the type of hormones:

**Estrogen-based HRT** (transfeminine)
- **Breasts** — very common during early breast development
- **Hips, buttocks, and thighs** — from fat redistribution to a more gynoid pattern
- **Lower abdomen** — if there’s any weight fluctuation

**Testosterone-based HRT** (transmasculine)
- **Shoulders and upper arms** — from increased muscle mass and broader shoulders
- **Back and lats** — muscle growth in the upper back
- **Thighs and abdomen** — from fat redistribution and general body recomposition

**General**
They can also appear on the **inner thighs**, **underarms**, or anywhere else experiencing rapid change. Genetics, skin elasticity, hydration, and the speed of changes play a big role in who gets them and how noticeable they are.
