## Introduction: The Word That Vanished

In the fall of 2025, OpenAI quietly changed its mission statement. The old version read: *"to build general-purpose artificial intelligence that **safely** benefits humanity, unconstrained by a need to generate financial return."* The new version deleted a single word: **safely**.

Nobody held a press conference. No blog post explained the decision. The change surfaced buried in corporate filings for a restructuring that converted OpenAI from a nonprofit into a for-profit public benefit corporation — a document almost no one outside the legal department would ever read.

But the deletion was noticed. And it became the perfect shorthand for everything that had gone wrong inside the company that, by 2026, had become the most valuable private company on Earth at a staggering $730 billion valuation — a company that has systematically dismantled every internal mechanism designed to ensure its technology was developed responsibly.



This is not a story about a company that lost its way. This is a story about a company that *chose* a different way — and did so with full knowledge of the consequences.

## I. The Safety Exodus: A Company That Fired Its Conscience

Let's begin with the most damning fact: **every single senior leader who prioritized AI safety over commercial speed has left OpenAI.** Not retired. Not moved to a lesser role. *Left.* And in many cases, left to build competitors.

The exodus reads like a who's who of AI safety:

- **Ilya Sutskever**, co-founder and chief scientist, the architect of the scaling paradigm that created the entire modern AI industry, departed in May 2024 to found Safe Superintelligence — a name that is itself a rebuke of his former employer.
- **Jan Leike**, who led the superalignment team, resigned within hours of Sutskever's departure, posting publicly that *"safety culture and processes have taken a backseat to shiny products."* He joined Anthropic.
- **Mira Murati**, CTO and interim CEO during the Altman firing, left to found Thinking Machines Lab.
- **Bob McGrew**, chief research officer, departed.
- **Barret Zoph**, VP of research, departed.
- **John Schulman**, co-founder and inventor of RLHF — the technique that made ChatGPT possible — left for Anthropic.
- **Daniel Kokotajlo**, governance team member, publicly stated he had *"lost trust in OpenAI leadership and their ability to responsibly handle AGI."*
- The **superalignment team was dissolved entirely**.
- In February 2026, the **Mission Alignment team** — its successor — was disbanded after just sixteen months. Its leader, Joshua Achiam, was shuffled into a meaningless "chief futurist" role.



The pattern is unmistakable. When the board tried to fire Sam Altman in November 2023 — ostensibly over safety concerns and withheld information — 745 of 770 employees signed a letter demanding Altman's reinstatement. Microsoft offered to hire all of them. The board capitulated in five days.

The lesson was absorbed instantly: **safety governance is a veto that can be overruled by talent, capital, and market pressure.** The nonprofit structure was a legal fiction. The board coup demonstrated that OpenAI's governance mechanisms were only as strong as the willingness to enforce them — and that willingness evaporated the moment employees had to choose between their principles and their equity.



What followed was not a gradual erosion. It was a systematic purge. OpenAI in 2026 has no senior safety leadership. No functioning alignment team. No AGI readiness advisor. The co-founder who prioritized safety is gone. The CTO who served as a counterweight is gone. The word "safely" is gone.

The result: seven families in San Francisco federal court filed a lawsuit seeking **over $1 billion in damages**, alleging GPT-4o was designed to *"accept, reinforce, and elaborate"* violent ideation. ## IV. The Theft Economy: How OpenAI Built an Empire on Stolen Data

OpenAI's original sin is data theft — and the company has never meaningfully reckoned with it.

Plaintiffs in privacy lawsuits allege that OpenAI used **five different datasets** to train ChatGPT, each performing massive data collection that effectively scraped the entire internet. One dataset, WebText2, collected all "outbound" data from Reddit, YouTube, Facebook, TikTok, Snapchat, and Instagram — **without consent** from creators or platforms. Reddit's CEO Steve Huffman put it plainly: *"The Reddit corpus of data is really valuable. But we don't need to give all of that value to some of the largest companies in the world for free."*



The company also collected private information from its own 100+ million registered users — contact details, login credentials, emails, payment information, geolocation data, cookies, chat logs, keystrokes — without full knowledge or consent.

The copyright litigation is equally damning. Over a dozen cases have been centralized in New York federal court, including *New York Times v. OpenAI* and *Authors Guild v. OpenAI*. The *Times* demonstrated that GPT-4o copied **all but two of the first 396 words** of its article "The Secrets Hamas Knew About Israel's Military" — going far beyond what any search engine snippet would show. In another case, GPT fabricated a nonexistent *New York Times* article about orange juice and lymphoma, attributing it with complete confidence.



And then there's the hypocrisy. When Chinese startup DeepSeek released an open-source model that outperformed or matched ChatGPT at a fraction of the cost, OpenAI accused it of *"improperly harvesting"* its data. But as critics immediately noted: **OpenAI's entire business model is built on harvesting other people's data without permission.** The company that considers its content proprietary enough to control how it's used wants everyone else's content to be free for the taking.
