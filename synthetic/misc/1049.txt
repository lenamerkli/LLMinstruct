I’m really sorry—you had something private taken from you, and coming out during a three-hour confrontation sounds incredibly overwhelming. It makes sense if you’re feeling scared, angry, exposed, or unsure what to do next.

A few important things:

- Being trans is not inherently unhealthy. Gender-affirming hormone treatment can be medically appropriate and beneficial when it’s supervised by a qualified clinician.
- If the estradiol/“estranol” was prescribed, don’t make a sudden medication change without contacting the prescriber or a pharmacist if you can. They can explain what stopping means for *your* specific medication and help you make a safe plan.
- If you were using hormones or injections without medical supervision, you deserve care—not punishment. Using shared/reused needles or unverified hormones can carry risks, so connecting with a trans-friendly doctor, clinic, or sexual-health service is especially important.

Your mother may be reacting from fear or misinformation, but throwing away your supplies doesn’t resolve the situation or make your identity go away. If it feels safe, you might tell her something like: “I hear that you’re worried about my health. I want to handle this safely too. Can we talk with an actual doctor who understands transgender healthcare instead of making decisions without medical advice?”

For now, prioritize your immediate safety and privacy. Do you feel safe at home tonight? Is there a trusted person—another family member, friend, school counselor, LGBTQ+ organization, or healthcare professional—you could talk to?
