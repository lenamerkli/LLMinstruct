The Safeguard American Voter Eligibility Act, also known as the SAVE Act or SAVE America Act, is a proposed United States law requiring documented proof of U.S. citizenship when registering to vote in U.S. federal elections. The bill was introduced among immigration policies of the second Donald Trump administration, under claims that there was significant fraud in previous elections, including voting by undocumented immigrants. The bill has been controversial, as the claims of fraud have not been proven in court, and opponents claim that a larger goal is meant to disenfranchise eligible voters. As of June 2026, the Senate officially did not pass the bill.

Provisions

The bill defines "documentary proof of United States citizenship".[9]

Valid documents for verifying citizenship, as stated by the bill, are one of the following:[10][9]

    A form of identification issued consistent with the requirements of the REAL ID law that indicates citizenship;[a]
    A valid U.S. passport;[b]
    An official US military identification card together with a service record indicating a place of birth in the United States;
    A valid government-issued photo ID card that lists a place of birth in the United States;
    Any other valid government-issued photo ID card together with evidence of birth as a US citizen or naturalization, such as a US-issued birth certificate, a naturalization certificate, or a Consular Report of Birth Abroad (CRBA).

Non-enhanced driver's licenses or state ID cards, U.S. military ID cards, and tribal identification documents would not be sufficient alone since they do not indicate citizenship status and can be issued to non-citizen legal residents of the United States.[13] Birth or naturalization certificates with names that do not match current identification could also not be used.[14]

The bill states that those who register to vote by mail "shall not be registered to vote in an election for Federal office unless (...) documentary proof of United States citizenship" is presented in person. It would likely also curtail the use of automatic voter registration ("motor voter" laws), and online voter registration.[13]

For those who cannot provide the documentary evidence required by the bill listed above, the bill would mandate each state to establish a process by which an applicant can sign an attestation that they are a US citizen, and a state or local official would then make a determination. Where there are discrepancies between documents presented, each state would have to provide a process wherein the applicant could submit additional documentation to establish their citizenship.[9]

The bill does not authorize federal funding for the new state responsibilities it creates, and it includes no phase-in period.[9]

The bill requires the removal of suspected non-citizens from existing voter rolls, though it does not require prior notification of erroneous removals.[15]

## The Double Standard at Play
The meme draws a parallel between two constitutional rights—voting and bearing arms—to suggest hypocrisy. While Republicans support the SAVE Act's strict documentation requirements for **voting** (citing concerns about election integrity and non-citizen fraud), they would reject identical barriers for **gun purchases** because:

1.  **Second Amendment Primacy:** Republicans generally treat the right to bear arms as a fundamental constitutional right that should not be burdened by "excessive bureaucracy." Requirements like in-person appearances with original citizenship documents, exact name matching, and waiting periods to fix clerical errors would be characterized as unconstitutional infringements that create "barriers to self-defense."

2.  **Opposition to Waiting Periods/Costs:** The image highlights costs for replacing documents and waiting periods. Gun rights advocates consistently oppose poll-tax-style fees and mandatory waiting periods for firearms, arguing they disproportionately burden low-income citizens and delay the exercise of a constitutional right.

3.  **"Motor Voter" vs. Automatic Registration:** Just as the SAVE Act would curtail automatic voter registration ("motor voter"), applying these rules to guns would effectively ban instant background checks or online FFL (Federal Firearms License) transfers—policies Republicans have generally opposed.

## Why Republicans See a Difference
Proponents of the SAVE Act would argue the analogy is false because:
*   **Nature of the Right:** Voting is a *civic participation* right (limited to citizens and regulated by states), while gun ownership is viewed as an *individual liberty* right protected against government infringement.
*   **Fraud Prevention vs. Defense:** They argue strict voter ID prevents fraud (non-citizens voting), whereas strict gun documentation only burdens law-abiding citizens (since criminals don't follow paperwork rules).
*   **Frequency:** Voting is periodic (every 2-4 years for most), while self-defense is viewed as an immediate, ongoing necessity.

## The Political Reality
As of June 2026, the SAVE Act had not passed the Senate. **Bottom line:** The image is rhetorical—designed to make Republican support for the SAVE Act look inconsistent with their broader "anti-bureaucracy" stance on constitutional rights.
