## Float16 vs Float32: Key Differences

### **Bit Layout**

**Float32 (Single Precision)**
- 1 sign bit
- 8 exponent bits
- 23 mantissa bits
- Total: 32 bits (4 bytes)

**Float16 (Half Precision)**
- 1 sign bit
- 5 exponent bits
- 10 mantissa bits
- Total: 16 bits (2 bytes)

### **Numerical Range & Precision**

| Aspect | Float16 | Float32 |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Max value** | 65,504 | ~3.4 × 10³⁸ |
| **Min positive normal** | 6.10 × 10⁻⁵ | ~1.2 × 10⁻³⁸ |
| **Decimal precision** | ~3-4 digits | ~7-8 digits |
| **Machine epsilon** | 0.00097 | ~1.2 × 10⁻⁷ |

### **Trade-offs**

**Float16 Advantages:**
- ✅ 50% memory usage
- ✅ Faster computation on GPUs with Tensor Cores
- ✅ Higher throughput (2x ops in same bandwidth)
- ✅ Reduced power consumption

**Float16 Disadvantages:**
- ❌ Limited range (overflow/underflow risk)
- ❌ Lower precision (rounding errors)
- ❌ Not suitable for accumulating small values
- ❌ Limited CPU support

### **Common Use Cases**

**Float16:**
- Deep learning inference
- Neural network training (with mixed precision)
- Graphics/gaming shaders
- Edge AI devices

**Float32:**
- Scientific computing
- Financial calculations
- Training (when precision critical)
- General CPU computations

### **Mixed Precision Training**
Modern approach combines both:
- Store weights/activations in float16
- Compute gradients in float32
- Use loss scaling to prevent underflow
