Of course! The Dutch text on the sign is a joke, written in a very informal, cutesy, and intentionally misspelled way, often referred to as "uwu-taal" or "owo-speak" in Dutch internet culture.

Here is a line-by-line translation:

**Original Dutch:**
> oepsie woepsie!
> de trein is stukkie wukkie!
> we zijn heul hard aan t werk om dit te make
> mss kan je beter fwietsen
> owo

**English Translation:**
> oopsie woopsie!
> the train is broken-woken!
> we are vewy hard at work to fix this
> maybe you can better cycle
> owo

---

**Breakdown of the slang:**

*   **Oepsie woepsie:** A cutesy, baby-talk version of "Oeps!" (Oops!).
*   **Stukkie wukkie:** A playful, rhyming misspelling of "stukkie" (a diminutive of "broken").
*   **Heul:** A deliberate misspelling of "heel" (very).
*   **Aan t werk:** A common colloquialism for "aan het werk" (at work).
*   **Make:** Using the English word "make" instead of the Dutch "maken" (to make/do).
*   **Mss:** Abbreviation for "misschien" (maybe).
*   **Fwietsen:** A cutesy misspelling of "fietsen" (to cycle/bike), where 'f' is often replaced with 'fw'.
*   **Owo:** An emoticon often used in this type of slang to convey cuteness.
