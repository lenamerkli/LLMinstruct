Here is the breakdown of the joke, explained for someone who doesn't drive:

**The Context: Tire Pressure**
Cars have a system called TPMS (Tire Pressure Monitoring System) that tells the driver how much air is in the tires. This is measured in **PSI** (Pounds per Square Inch).
*   **Normal:** For a standard passenger car, the correct tire pressure is usually between **30 and 35 PSI**.
*   **The Image:** The dashboard in the picture shows the tires are at **93, 97, 96, and 94 PSI**. This is roughly **triple** the normal amount. The tires are dangerously overinflated.

**The First Tweet (The Setup)**
The user (@bennynotblanco) is complaining: *"no matter how much air i put it won't go 100."*
*   **The Joke:** They are treating the tire pressure like a video game stat or a high score that needs to hit a perfect "100." They don't realize that hitting 100 PSI would actually be catastrophic. They are pumping air into already rock-hard tires trying to get a round number.

**The Second Tweet (The Punchline)**
The user (@c0mmandderr) replies: *"Mf gon hit a pothole and get in a plane accident."*
*   **Why it's funny:** Tires are filled with air so they act like shock absorbers. When you hit a bump, the tire squishes a little bit to cushion the ride.
*   Because these tires are at 90+ PSI, they are basically solid balls of rubber. They have zero "give" or squish.
*   If you hit a pothole with tires that hard, the car won't absorb the shock—it will **bounce violently** into the air. The joke exaggerates this by saying the car will bounce so high and uncontrollably that it will be like a plane crash.

**Summary**
The humor comes from the fact that the driver is clueless about basic car maintenance (trying to get to 100 PSI) and the responder is jokingly predicting a violent, airborne disaster because of it.
