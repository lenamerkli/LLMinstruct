§u§
What formats exist to save 3D objects?
§a§
There are many file formats used to save 3D objects, each with different strengths depending on the use case (3D printing, game development, CAD, animation, web, etc.). Here's an overview of the most common ones:

## General-Purpose / Interchange Formats

- **OBJ (.obj)** — One of the oldest and most widely supported formats. Stores geometry (vertices, faces, normals, texture coordinates) but no animation. Often paired with an **.mtl** file for materials. Great for static models.
- **FBX (.fbx)** — A popular proprietary format (owned by Autodesk) supporting geometry, materials, textures, animation, skeletons, and cameras. Widely used in game and film pipelines.
- **glTF / GLB (.gltf, .glb)** — A modern, open standard called the "JPEG of 3D." Efficient for web and real-time applications; supports PBR materials, animation, and scenes. GLB is the binary version.
- **COLLADA (.dae)** — An open XML-based format for exchanging assets between applications. Supports geometry, materials, animation, and physics.
- **USD / USDZ (.usd, .usdz)** — Universal Scene Description, developed by Pixar. Increasingly the industry standard for complex scenes and pipelines; USDZ is used for AR on Apple devices.

## 3D Printing Formats

- **STL (.stl)** — The classic 3D printing format. Describes surface geometry only as triangles—no color, texture, or units. Simple and universal.
- **3MF (.3mf)** — A modern replacement for STL that supports color, materials, and more metadata in a single file.
- **AMF (.amf)** — An XML-based format supporting color, materials, and curved surfaces.

## CAD / Engineering Formats

- **STEP (.stp, .step)** — A standardized format (ISO 10303) for precise solid models, widely used in engineering and manufacturing.
- **IGES (.igs, .iges)** — An older CAD interchange standard for surfaces and curves.
- **Parasolid (.x_t), SAT (.sat)** — Kernel-based formats used by various CAD systems.
- **Native CAD formats** — e.g., **.sldprt** (SolidWorks), **.ipt** (Inventor), **.dwg/.dxf** (AutoCAD).

## Application-Specific / Scene Formats

- **BLEND (.blend)** — Blender's native format, storing entire scenes including objects, materials, animation, and more.
- **MAX (.max)** / **MA, MB (.ma, .mb)** — Native formats for 3ds Max and Maya, respectively.
- **C4D (.c4d)** — Cinema 4D's native format.

## Point Cloud / Scanning Formats

- **PLY (.ply)** — Polygon File Format, often used for scanned data; supports vertex colors and properties.
- **PCD, LAS, E57** — Used for point clouds from 3D scanning and LiDAR.

## Quick Guidance on Choosing

- **Game engines (Unity/Unreal):** FBX or glTF
- **Web/AR:** glTF/GLB or USDZ
- **3D printing:** STL or 3MF
- **Engineering/CAD:** STEP or native CAD formats
- **Film/VFX pipelines:** USD, FBX, or Alembic (.abc)
- **Simple static model sharing:** OBJ

Based on your requirements—**geometry + textures + point clouds + suitable for a whole real-life town**—you need a format that can handle large-scale, often georeferenced data with mixed content types. Here are the best candidates:

## Top Recommendations

### 1. **3D Tiles (.json + tiles)** — Best for towns/cities
This is an open OGC standard specifically designed for streaming **massive geospatial datasets**. It's arguably the best fit for your exact use case:
- ✅ **Geometry** (meshes via embedded glTF)
- ✅ **Textures** (PBR materials through glTF)
- ✅ **Point clouds** (native tile type)
- ✅ **Real-life town** — built for entire cities, photogrammetry, terrain, and LiDAR. It uses hierarchical "tiling" so a browser/engine only loads what's visible, which is essential at town scale.

Widely used with Cesium, Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles, and Unreal/Unity. This is what powers many digital twin and city-scale visualizations.

### 2. **USD / USDZ (.usd, .usdz)** — Best all-in-one scene format
- ✅ Geometry (meshes, subdivision surfaces)
- ✅ Textures (full PBR materials)
- ✅ Point clouds (via `UsdGeomPoints`)
- ✅ Can compose large, complex scenes through layering/referencing—good for assembling a town from many assets.

Less inherently "geospatial" than 3D Tiles (no built-in georeferencing/streaming for huge areas), but extremely capable and increasingly an industry standard.

### 3. **E57 (.e57)** — If point clouds are the priority
- ✅ Point clouds (excellent, the standard for laser scanning)
- ✅ Textures/imagery (stores panoramic images alongside scans)
- ⚠️ Geometry (limited—primarily a scan/point-cloud container, not for arbitrary meshes)

Great if your town representation is primarily scan-based, but weaker for authored mesh geometry.

## Honorable Mentions

- **glTF/GLB** — Supports geometry, textures, and *does* have a point cloud primitive mode, but it isn't designed for town-scale streaming on its own (which is exactly why 3D Tiles wraps glTF for that purpose).
- **CityGML / CityJSON** — Purpose-built for representing cities/towns with semantic building data, but point cloud support is weak; better for structured urban models than mixed point-cloud + mesh data.
