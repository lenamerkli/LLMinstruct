Short answer: **yes**—the wording is aimed at **students being given access to AI tools for schoolwork**, not at banning computers that contain low-level predictive logic. So **CPU branch prediction would not be the target**. That’s an inference from the official wording and context.

The June 19, 2026 Norwegian government press release describes this as a **“new national recommendation”** and says: **“Fra høsten skal elevene på 1.–7. trinn skjermes fra å selv bruke kunstig intelligens i skolearbeidet”**—roughly, “From autumn, pupils in grades 1–7 are to be shielded from using AI themselves in schoolwork.” It then gives the key rule: **“På 1.–7. trinn skal elevene i all hovedsak ikke gis tilgang til kunstig intelligens”**—roughly, “In grades 1–7, pupils should, as a general rule, not be given access to AI.”

Two scope clues matter a lot. First, the text is about **use in schoolwork / instruction**, not about hardware in general. Second, the same release explicitly keeps room for exceptions: students who **need AI-based tools in teaching** should still get them, for example in language learning or individually adapted instruction. That makes this a **student-use policy**, not a blanket ban on any device containing anything one might loosely call AI.

The surrounding guidance from Norway’s education directorate points the same way. It discusses AI in schools in terms of **generative AI, language models, chatbots, and digital teaching tools**, and says schools may use such tools if they fit the curriculum and privacy/security requirements. Reuters also summarized the move as a near ban on **“generative AI tools”** for elementary pupils.

So if your question is, “Would a literal reading also outlaw CPUs with branch predictors, even ML-ish ones?” my answer is **no**. The operative phrases are about pupils **using AI themselves in schoolwork** and being **given access to AI**. A branch predictor inside a CPU is not the kind of tool a pupil is being given access to for school tasks; it’s just an internal implementation detail of the computer. That is an interpretation, but it is strongly supported by the official context.
