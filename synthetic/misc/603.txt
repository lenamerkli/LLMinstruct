# Both Equations Are Correct

These are actually the **same relationship** written in two equivalent forms — they are inverse transformations of each other.

## What Each Represents

These are the **relativistic velocity addition formulas** from special relativity:

**First equation:** Transforms velocity from frame S to frame S'
$$u' = \frac{u - v}{1 - \frac{uv}{c^2}}$$

**Second equation:** Transforms velocity from frame S' to frame S
$$u = \frac{u' + v}{1 + \frac{u'v}{c^2}}$$

## The Setup

- **S** = stationary reference frame
- **S'** = frame moving with velocity **v** relative to S
- **u** = object's velocity measured in frame S
- **u'** = object's velocity measured in frame S'

## Verification

You can algebraically solve the first equation for *u* and obtain the second equation (or vice versa). The sign change (minus ↔ plus) reflects the reversal of perspective between frames.

## Which to Use?

| If you know... | And want to find... | Use |
|----------------|---------------------|-----|
| u (velocity in S) | u' (velocity in S') | First equation |
| u' (velocity in S') | u (velocity in S) | Second equation |
