**AO3 fic** stands for **“Archive of Our Own fanfiction”** — specifically, a story (or “fic,” short for “fan fiction”) published on the **Archive of Our Own (AO3)**, which is one of the largest and most popular free online platforms for hosting fan-created stories.

---

### What is AO3?

- **Full name**: Archive of Our Own
- **URL**: https://archiveofourown.org/
- **Founded by**: The Organization for Transformative Works (OTW), a nonprofit founded in 2008.
- **Purpose**: To provide a safe, community-run archive where fans can share their creative works — including fanfiction, art, videos, etc. — without fear of censorship or commercial exploitation.
- **Content**: Mostly fanfiction based on existing media (TV shows, movies, books, games, real people/celebrities, original characters, etc.), but also includes transformative works like meta-analysis, poetry, and more.

---

### What does being “in an AO3 fic” mean in this context?

In Jasmine’s tweet:

> *“...I think that’s the closest I’ve been to being in an AO3 fic.”*

She’s making a **humorous/self-deprecating joke**. She’s describing a real-life situation — her doctor friend checking her chest with a stethoscope while she was still pre-transition and living as male (“boymoding”) — that feels so oddly intimate, dramatic, or emotionally charged that it reminds her of tropes found in **transgender-themed fanfiction** often written on AO3.

Many AO3 fics explore themes like:
- Trans identity discovery
- Medical transitions (hormones, surgeries, check-ups)
- Secret identities coming out
- Intimate moments between friends who don’t know each other’s gender history
- Doctor/patient dynamics (especially if romantic or emotionally tense)

So when she says “closest I’ve been to being in an AO3 fic,” she means:
👉 *This moment felt so cinematic, emotional, or trope-y that it could easily be a scene from a trans-themed fanfic.*

It’s not literal — she’s not actually inside a fictional story — but rather, life imitated art (or internet fiction).

### Why AO3 is culturally significant

- Known for its **robust tagging system** (users tag content with warnings, pairings, genres, triggers, etc.)
- Very inclusive of LGBTQ+ content, especially trans/non-binary narratives
- Popular among marginalized communities for expressing identities safely
- Often referenced in memes, tweets, and pop culture (like this one!)
