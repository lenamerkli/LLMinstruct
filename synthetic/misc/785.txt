 A breakaway female cyclist was forced to stop during a prestigious race in Belgium after she started to catch up with the men’s competition, which had started 10 minutes earlier.

Swiss cyclist Nicole Hanselmann described it as an “awkward moment” when she was held up in Saturday’s Omloop Het Nieuwsblad race after she broke away from the women’s pack and caught up with the men’s support vehicles.

She had developed a two-minute lead 30 kilometers (18 miles) into the 120-kilometer (75-mile) race when officials asked the 27-year old former Switzerland road champion – and the women’s race – to stop at a level crossing until the gap with the men’s race was restored.

The racing event’s official Twitter account reported the “neutralization of our women race at railroad crossing in Sint-Denijs-Boekel… due to a very slow mens race.”

Is this true?
§a§
Yes — the incident is real.

It happened at the **2019 Omloop Het Nieuwsblad** women’s race in Belgium. Swiss rider **Nicole Hanselmann** attacked early and built a lead of about **two minutes**. Because the women’s race had started only **10 minutes after** the men’s race, and the men’s race was moving slowly, Hanselmann caught up to the **back of the men’s race convoy/support vehicles**.

Race officials then **neutralized/stopped the women’s race** near a level crossing in **Sint-Denijs-Boekel** to re-establish a safe gap between the two races. The official race account did indeed describe it as being due to a “very slow men’s race.”

One nuance: she did not catch the front of the men’s race or race alongside the male riders; she caught the **rear caravan/support vehicles**. After the pause, she was allowed to restart with her previous advantage, though she was later caught by the women’s peloton.
