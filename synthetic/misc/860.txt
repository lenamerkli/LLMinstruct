§a§
The hypocrisy claim is about **inconsistent principles**.

In the image, PragerU is saying two things:

1. **About YouTube:**
   “Big Tech shouldn’t restrict our content; this is censorship.”

2. **About the baker case:**
   “If a private business doesn’t want to serve you, go somewhere else. Don’t force them.”

Why people call that hypocritical:

- **YouTube is also a private business.**
- So if PragerU’s principle is really **“private businesses should decide what they host or serve”**, then they should accept YouTube’s moderation the same way they say gay customers should accept a baker’s refusal.
- But instead, they defend private discretion in one case and condemn it in the other because **the outcome hurts them**.

So the accusation is not just “they disagree with YouTube.” It’s that they seem to apply **free-market/private-property principles selectively**:

- **When conservatives want freedom to refuse service:** “It’s their business.”
- **When a platform restricts conservative content:** “This is unfair censorship.”

A possible defense of PragerU would be:

- They may argue there’s a difference between **refusing to create custom expressive work** (a cake message) and **suppressing speech on a dominant platform**.
- Or they may argue Big Tech acts more like a **public square** than an ordinary business.

But the meme’s point is that **PragerU often presents itself as strongly pro-private-business**, then objects when a private company uses that same freedom against them.

So, in short: **they’re called hypocritical because they support “private business rights” when it helps their side, but reject those same rights when those businesses restrict them.**
